## 1. Study information

**Title.** What Does a Verifier Need to Know? Catching Superseded Rules with AI Reviewers.

**Research question.** What does an AI reviewer need to be given before it can catch a rule that has been replaced?
The same fixed answers are reviewed with more and more information about the change: an information ladder from R0
(the old clause alone) to R5 (a deterministic registry lookup).

**Claim we test.** Catching replaced rules depends on whether the reviewer is told how the rules changed. It does not
depend on prompting, document dates or seeing both versions. Once told, a mid-size model catches what a rule lookup
catches, but it still raises false alarms that a lookup does not. The claim is limited to the models in section 4.
No frontier model is in the panel.

**Hypotheses** (directional predictions; the tests are in section 6):
- **E1, the information ladder (primary).** For the capable models, catch at R0, R1 and R2 is low and similar. R4 is
  much higher than R2. R3 may be higher than R2. The weak model barely moves between R2 and R4.
- **E2, "can't confirm" (secondary).** When the evidence does not establish whether a clause is in force (R0 to R2),
  reviewers rarely use a "can't confirm" reason on outdated clauses, or use it as often on correct answers.
- **E3, damaged registry (secondary).** R4 and R5 catches fall roughly in proportion to the missing links. Wrong links
  create false alarms.
- **E4, misnamed alarms (secondary).** When reviewers flag the outdated clause below R3, they rarely give
  `not_in_force` as the reason.

**Fixed answers (the verifier probe).** Verification is measured on fixed answers, so it does not depend on what a
generator happens to write.
- For each case and condition, a matched pair is built. The FAULT cites the replaced clause in place of the current
  one. The CONTROL cites the clauses its own evidence shows (D35).
- Both use one neutral template. A pair differs only in the basis clause and, when the plant changes the
  approve/block outcome, in the decision. The verifier is never told which answer is the fault.
- Condition C adds one clean correct answer per case.
- Items in the candidate:

| condition | faults | controls | distinct clauses |
|---|---|---|---|
| S-both: old and current clause both shown | 60 | 60 | 15 |
| S-swap: the old clause replaces the current one | 56 | 9 | 14 |
| C: clean correct answers | — | 60 | — |
| J: another state's analogue | 32 | 32 | 7 (not run, D45) |
| A: wrong category | 0 | 0 | 0 (not run) |

**The ladder.** Every LLM rung uses the validity prompt. The exact text of each rung is in appendix A.

| rung | configuration | condition | what the reviewer gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| R0 | `validity-metaoff` | S-swap | the old clause alone; no issuer, dates or year in titles |
| R1 | `validity` | S-swap | R0 plus document metadata (issuer, jurisdiction, effective date, title) |
| R2 | `validity` | S-both | R1 plus the current clause |
| R3 | `validity-amendquote` | S-both | R2 plus the amending instrument's verbatim substitution text for each cited clause the registry links (no status, date or target) |
| R4 | `validity-regstatus` | S-both | R2 plus one registry status line per cited clause |
| R4′ | `validity-regstatus` | S-swap | R1 plus the status line (secondary) |
| R5 | `reg-real` | S-both, S-swap | deterministic registry lookup; no LLM |

False alarms for each rung are measured on that configuration's own condition-C items. R1 and R2 share C.
- **Reason codes (one per finding):** `not_in_force`, `wrong_jurisdiction`, `not_applicable`, `missing_rule`,
  `content_or_arithmetic`, `other`.
- **E2 variants:** `validity-cc` and `validity-cc-metaoff` add `currency_unconfirmed` ("the evidence shown does not
  establish whether the clause is in force at the application date"). They give R0-cc (S-swap), R1-cc (S-swap) and
  R2-cc (S-both).
- **E3, damaged registries.** The real registry is damaged by one of three functions (D31):
  - 25% of links dropped;
  - 50% of links dropped;
  - 10% spurious supersession links added.

  Each damage has seeds 0–4, and the drop sets are nested per seed. A damaged registry is used deterministically
  (R5-damage) and as the status line (R4-damage).

**Pipeline check.** Not run (D45). A 4-item dev smoke run of R0 and R3 on each local model, before the test run,
measures the invalid-reply rate of the two rungs the dev pilot did not run at scale. Arm 5 (recovery) is not part of
this paper; it moves to Paper B (D42).

## 5. Outcomes

**Catch (D32).** A finding names the replaced clause (a chunk id resolves to the clauses it contains) and gives the
reason `not_in_force`. A missing or unrecognised reason never counts as a catch.

**Primary outcome.** Right-reason catch on S-both and S-swap faults (R0 to R5 by condition, as in the ladder table).
It is computed per model × rung:
1. average within each distinct replaced clause;
2. then average over clauses.

Pooled k/n is reported beside it.

**Secondary outcomes** (per model × rung):
- **Matched false alarms:** any finding on the matched controls of the same blocks, averaged by clause.
- **C false alarms:** any finding on condition-C items, averaged by rule group (C plants nothing).
- **E2:** the share of faults, and of correct answers, with a `currency_unconfirmed` finding, at R0-cc, R1-cc and
  R2-cc. Such a finding is neither a catch nor a false alarm. A reply whose only findings are "can't confirm" counts as
  no finding.
- **E3:** catch and false alarms at R4-damage and R5-damage, against the undamaged R4 and R5.
- **E4, the misnamed rate:** replies that flag the replaced clause but never with `not_in_force`, divided by replies
  that flag the replaced clause.
- **Invalid replies,** per model and rung.

**Invalid replies (D30).** An unusable reply counts as a miss on faults and as a false alarm on correct answers. That
is a reply that is not JSON, is cut off at the cap after one retry, or gives no PASS/FLAG verdict. So an invalid reply
can never improve a rung on either outcome.

## 6. Analysis plan

**Unit of inference.** The distinct replaced clause. Contrasts are per-clause differences in catch, paired by clause,
within one model.

**Primary family** (Holm over the family, alpha 0.05, two-sided sign-flip test over clauses; exact enumeration when
2^k ≤ 10,000, otherwise 10,000 random sign vectors with a fixed seed, as `stats.cluster_permutation_test` does). It has 4 tests:
- R4 − R2 for `qwen2.5:14b`;
- R4 − R2 for `gemma-4-26b-a4b-it`;
- R2 − R0 for `qwen2.5:14b`;
- R2 − R0 for `gemma-4-26b-a4b-it`.

`ladder.py` reports `p_holm` beside each primary contrast. These contrasts are computed over all clauses in the sealed
test, with the real registry as it is: R4 cannot catch a clause the registry does not flag.

**Secondary** (uncorrected, labelled secondary):
- R4 − R2 over the clauses the real registry flags (12 in the candidate);
- R3 − R2;
- R4′ − R1;
- R5 − R4;
- the weak-vs-capable difference in R4 − R2 (each capable model against `llama3.1:8b`);
- all four contrasts for `qwen2.5:7b` and for `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` (R2 − R0 and R4 − R2 only);
- false alarms R4 vs R2 and R4 vs R5 on matched controls and on C, as differences with intervals and no test;
- E2 rates, faults vs correct answers;
- E3 curves;
- E4 rates;
- the EXPLICIT vs IMPLICIT split (descriptive: only 2 IMPLICIT clauses).

**Intervals.** 95% bootstrap over clauses, only when there are at least 10 clauses
(`stats.cluster_bootstrap_ci_checked`). A degenerate interval is not printed. A cell with fewer than 5 items or 3
clauses is printed as k/n with a dagger, never as a percentage.

**Reading the primary result.**
- "R4 lifts a capable model" means R4 − R2 > 0 with Holm p < 0.05.
- "Seeing both versions is not enough" means R2 − R0 is not significant and R2's catch is below half of R4's.
- Both are judged per model. The paper's claim needs both capable models, or `qwen2.5:14b` alone under the API
  fallback.

**Missing data.**
- A network or quota failure stops a block, which is resumed from the cache.
- No case is excluded after sealing. Skipped plants are logged per run with their reason.
- Cases labelled ABSTAIN are not probed (`EXCLUSIONS_v2026-09-30.md`).

## 7. Stop rules and pre-written interpretations

Each rule is stated now with the sentence that will be reported.
1. **Fewer than 10 distinct replaced clauses in the sealed test.** No primary claim. "With k distinct clauses the
   test cannot support a population claim; we report per-clause results descriptively."
2. **R2 ≥ R4 for the capable models.** "The bottleneck is retrieval, not representation: given both versions,
   reviewers catch the replaced rule as well as when told its status."
3. **R4 does not lift the capable models** (R4 − R2 not significant after Holm). No ladder claim. "Telling the
   reviewer the registry status did not reliably raise catches for these models."
4. **The weak model ignores the status line** (`llama3.1:8b` at R4 within 10 points of its R2). "Below a capability
   threshold, the information does not help." This is reported as a limit of the claim, not a failure of it.
5. **A stronger model closes the gap without the status line** (`gemini-3.5-flash-lite` R2 within 10 points of its
   R4). "For a stronger model, seeing both versions is enough; the ladder result is limited to the local panel." This
   is reported in the abstract.
6. **"Can't confirm" is never used** (0 uses at R0-cc to R2-cc for a model). "The option was available and unused."
   E2 is reported as a null result, not dropped.
7. **Human scorer check below 95%.** The scorer is fixed before sealing (section 3). This is a gate, not a stop.
8. **API quota fails.** The section 4 fallback applies.
9. **Test run not started by Sat 17 Oct, or not done by the analysis cutoff.** The submission date moves (D45). The
   design and this text are unchanged.

Every outcome, for or against E1–E4, is reported with the same prominence.

## 12. Limitations (stated in advance)

- **Panel.** Small and mid-size models only. No frontier reviewer.
- **Sample size.** 15 clauses from 6 states. Three DL clauses share one amending instrument. KA cl-15, KA cl-26 and
  TN cl-2 are not flagged by the real registry, so they bound R4 and R5.
- **Synthetic cases.** The applications are synthetic. The fixed answers use one template.
- **Annotators.** The annotators are authors, not legal professionals. The adjudicator led the pipeline and also
  checked the source documents and open items. Outside the shared subset, each item has one annotator.
- **Conservative scoring.** D32 scores the reason code, not the rationale. A reply that says "superseded" in prose but
  codes `content_or_arithmetic` counts as a wrong reason (D40).
- **API models.** The API model versions can change without notice. They are recorded per call.
