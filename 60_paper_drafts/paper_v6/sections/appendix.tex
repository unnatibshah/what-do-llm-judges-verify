\section{Case study: Indian rooftop-solar regulation}
\label{app:case}

\noindent\fbox{\parbox{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule}{\small
\textbf{Status: descriptive only.} The test was first developed on this setting, where no curated consolidation
exists and the amendment record had to be extracted from the documents by an AI pipeline. Its answer key failed the
quality checks planned for it: a person applying the right-reason rule agreed with the automatic scorer on
\ScorerCheckPctObserved{} of dev replies, against a planned threshold of 95\%, and when people read whole case files,
many of the fixed ``correct'' answers turned out to be incomplete. None of its numbers is used as evidence for the
hypotheses or findings of \S\ref{sec:results}. It is reported because its failures, annotation disagreements and
negative results show what the test needs from an uncurated amendment record; its planned analyses and readings are
kept as they were fixed, so that nothing is selected after the fact. The full report (reference data and their audit,
conditions, planned analyses and results, readings fixed in advance, prompts, per-clause results, annotation
agreement, deviations from the plan and the data statement) is in the supplementary material.}}
\medskip

Two consequences follow for reading the numbers below. False alarms on correct answers cannot be taken as the judge's
errors. For example, Claude Opus~4.5 raised some finding on \RobFlaggedClaudeOpusRFour{} correct answers when given the
status line, but most of its findings were not about validity (\RobNonValidityClaudeOpus), and only a corrected key
could say whether they were wrong. Likewise, the large differences between any-finding and right-reason catches in this
study are real outputs on this key, but we do not use them to support \S\ref{sec:res-scoring}, which rests on the RFC
data. The text below is carried over from the study's own report, written before the RFC study, and uses its terms
