\section{Limitations and threats to validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Two draws, few items.} Each draw gives each judge 24 gap faults; the two draws share 12,
so the gap result rests on 36 distinct links. The drop held on both draws for every judge, but the pilot showed how much
a judge's result can depend on which links are dropped (\S\ref{sec:res-gaps}), and differences between judges of a few
items remain within what such variation could produce. The presentation conditions were run on the first draw only,
and Claude's presentation effects are not significant.

\paragraph{Single runs and decoding.} Hosted judges ran once each per prompt, through a proxy that sets and does not
report temperature and seed. Two checks bound the variation this allows: in the pilot, a repeated block changed 30 of
72 replies but only one catch, and requesting the pilot's prompts again changed the score of at most
\EOpusReaskChanged{} of a judge's \EOpusReaskItems{} items (\S\ref{sec:studies}). The second draw reuses the first
draw's text-only replies, and Gemma's client sometimes retried a cut-off reply without the API's JSON mode.

\paragraph{The conflict code.} The context with a conflict code was added after the main results existed, in one
wording, on one draw, without a planned test. It shows that one judge uses such a code often and the others rarely or
never; it cannot show how other wordings of such a code would fare.

\paragraph{The decoys are a partial control.} They are matched on dates, title similarity and, for most pairs, IETF
area, but not on abstract overlap, which differs sharply between replacements and updates. Some decoys are new
protocol versions, for which flagging the older RFC is a defensible reading of the question even though the
publisher's record says it was not replaced. The similarity analysis is exploratory, post hoc and small.

\paragraph{Construct.} The evidence is titles, dates and abstracts, not full documents. The error tested is always the
citation of a whole replaced document; partial amendments and chains are not covered. Links are dropped at random,
while real records fail in patterns (recent amendments, particular issuers). The question names the old document's
title, which may provoke some of the non-currency findings on controls. Two of the 24 gap faults are weaker items:
RFC~7142 (``Reclassification of RFC~1142 to Historic'') and RFC~6082 (``\ldots RFC~2482 is Historic'') name the
old RFC in their titles, so the replacement is stated rather than inferred, and as reclassification notices they do
not themselves specify the protocol, so the controls citing them are weaker. They account for two of Gemini's lost
catches and one each of GPT-5.5's and Claude's (Table~\ref{tab:e123-items}); without them every judge still lost
catches and gained none (Claude 7 lost, two-sided exact $p$ = .016). Under the damaged record Gemini missed both,
even though the newer RFC's title states the replacement.

\paragraph{Generalisation.} One corpus of technical standards, chosen because its publisher maintains an exact
record, and four judges. Legal collections add problems this test does not address, as the case study illustrates:
partial amendments, records extracted by machine, and answer keys that people disagree about. The title of the paper
asks a general question; the answer we give is limited to these judges, prompts and documents. In this corpus a
replacing RFC usually repeats its predecessor's title and abstract, so text-only catches are comparatively easy;
where amendments look little like the original, as in many contracts and regulations, both the text-only baseline
and what a record suppresses are untested.

\paragraph{Mechanism.} The presentation conditions keep the record fixed and vary single features, one prompt variant
each. They can show that presentation changed what a judge did; they cannot show why.

\paragraph{Provenance.} The harness and the text were produced with an AI assistant from the same developer as one of
the judges, Claude Opus~4.5 (\S\ref{sec:ai-use}). All judges received identical prompts and items, and the gold comes
from the publisher. After the pilot, the plan was drafted with that assistant, then reviewed and amended by the authors; it
was recorded internally rather than registered publicly.
