LLM judges often check, against an amendment record, whether the documents an answer cites are still in force. Such records are rarely complete. From a table of replacements, our test generates matched answers citing a replaced document, its
successor, the replaced document before its replacement, and an older document that a newer one updated without
replacing; it removes links from the record and scores each finding by its stated reason. On \ENLinks{}
replacements from the IETF RFC index, judges given a correct record caught nearly every replaced citation, as a lookup would. On the 24 faults whose link we removed, every judge caught more from the texts alone
than with the record: Gemini~3.1~Pro \EGeminiGapTexts{} against \EGeminiGapDamaged, GPT-5.5
\EGPTGapTexts{} against \EGPTGapDamaged, Claude Opus~4.5 \EOpusGapTexts{} against \EOpusGapDamaged{} and Gemma~4~26B
\EGemmaGapTexts{} against \EGemmaGapDamaged. The drop recurred on a second draw of removed links.
Most misses carried no finding; in an exploratory run with a conflict code, only Gemini used it often. Rewording the gap
as ``no replacement recorded'' did not help; presenting the record as an unverified note, or warning that it was incomplete, restored most lost catches
for Gemini, GPT-5.5 and Claude (significant after correction for the first two) but at most \EGemmaArmCaveatGained{} of \EGemmaHTwoaLost{} for
Gemma; where catches returned, so did false alarms on updates. With both texts, judges mostly told replacements from updates;
every update flagged had a near-identical title. A descriptive case study on Indian rooftop-solar rules is appended; we release the
harness.
